# Lesson 8: Common Tourist Pitfalls

These are the mistakes that cost visitors money, time or goodwill most often,
grouped by when you'll meet them.

## 1. Before you fly

| Pitfall | What to do instead |
|---------|-------------------|
| **No plug adapter.** Japan uses **type A** (two flat pins) at **100 V**. | Bring a type A adapter. Phone and laptop chargers (100–240 V) are fine, but 230 V-only hair dryers and straighteners won't work properly. |
| **Banned medicines.** Products containing stimulants, such as some **pseudoephedrine cold medicines**, **Vicks inhalers** and **amphetamine-based ADHD drugs**, are prohibited. | Check the Japanese health ministry's guidance before you travel. Bring prescriptions in their original packaging. Up to a month's supply of most prescription medicine needs no paperwork. |
| **Driving licence** | You need an **International Driving Permit (1949 Geneva type)**. Drivers from **Germany, France, Belgium, Switzerland and Monaco** need an official **Japanese translation** of their licence instead (from JAF). Driving is on the left. |
| **Peak seasons**: New Year (about 29 Dec–3 Jan), **Golden Week** (late April–early May) and **Obon** (mid-August) | Book transport and hotels early, or avoid these dates. Many shops and restaurants close over New Year. |
| **Weather**: rainy season (June–mid-July), typhoons (August–October), humid 35 °C summers | Pack a light umbrella, plan indoor alternatives, and drink plenty in summer. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable. |
| **Paperwork at the airport** | Fill in **Visit Japan Web** before arrival to get QR codes for immigration and customs. |
| **Assuming the JR Pass is a bargain**: its price rose sharply in 2023. | Compare it with single tickets using a fare calculator. For a simple Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka trip, single tickets are often cheaper. |

## 2. Money

| Pitfall | What to do instead |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Running out of cash**: small restaurants, temples, shrines, markets and some ticket machines take cash only. | Carry ¥10,000–20,000. Withdraw at **7-Eleven**, Japan Post, Lawson or FamilyMart ATMs, which accept foreign cards. |
| **Accepting the card terminal's offer to "pay in EUR"** (dynamic currency conversion) | Always choose to pay in **yen**. The conversion rate offered is usually poor. |
| **A heavy pile of coins**: ¥100 and ¥500 coins add up fast. | Use a coin purse, and spend coins at konbini and vending machines. Top up your IC card with them. |
| **Surprise extras**: prices shown **tax-excluded** (税抜), izakaya **otōshi** (a small starter you didn't order, ¥300–800 per person, really a cover charge), table charges, late-night taxi surcharge (+20% after 22:00) | Read 税込 / 税抜 on price tags. Otōshi is normal and not a scam. |
| **Street touts** (*kyakuhiki*) in Kabukichō, Roppongi or Namba offering bars or "girls" | Don't follow them. Bills of hundreds of thousands of yen are a known scam. |

## 3. Getting around

| Pitfall | What to do instead |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Missing the last train**: services stop around midnight, and taxis are expensive. | Check your **last connection** in the app before dinner. |
| **Big suitcases on the Shinkansen**: on the Tōkaidō, San'yō and Kyūshū lines, bags with total dimensions of 161–250 cm need a **seat with an oversized-baggage area** booked in advance. | Book it with your ticket, or send your luggage ahead. |
| **Hauling luggage through rush hour and station stairs** | Use **luggage forwarding** (*takkyūbin*, e.g. Yamato "TA-Q-BIN"): hotel to hotel, usually next day, from any konbini or hotel front desk. |
| **Getting lost in giant stations** (Shinjuku, Umeda, Tokyo) | Allow 10–15 minutes. Follow the **line colours** and **exit numbers**, not the crowd (see Lesson 6). |
| **Japanese addresses**: most streets have no names, and buildings are numbered by block. | Navigate with map pins, and save your hotel's name and address in Japanese to show taxi drivers. |
| **Over-planning**: distances in Tokyo are large, and transfers take time. | Plan **one area per half day**. Expect to walk 15,000–25,000 steps a day. |

## 4. Food & restaurants

| Pitfall | What to do instead |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Arriving after "L.O."** (last order), often 30–60 minutes before closing | Arrive well before closing time, especially for dinner. |
| **Assuming walk-ins are fine** at well-known sushi, kaiseki or popular restaurants | Reserve through your hotel or a booking site. Expect queues at famous ramen shops. |
| **Waiting to be served at a ticket-machine restaurant** | Buy a ticket (食券) from the machine at the entrance first, then hand it to staff. |
| **Monday closures**: many museums close on Mondays (or Tuesday after a holiday), and restaurants have a **定休日** (regular day off). | Check opening days before you go. |
| **Allergies and vegetarian diets**: fish stock (*dashi*) is in almost everything. | Carry a card in Japanese describing your diet (see Lesson 5). |

## 5. Sightseeing & behaviour

| Pitfall | What to do instead |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Huge crowds** at Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera, Arashiyama or Senso-ji | Go **early in the morning** (before 8:00) or late in the afternoon. |
| **Kyoto's Gion**: chasing geiko/maiko for photos, and entering private lanes (fines apply in some) | Watch respectfully from a distance and obey the "no photos" signs. |
| **Nara's deer** headbutt and bite when they see crackers. | Show your empty hands when you've finished feeding them, and keep paper maps and bags out of reach. |
| **Noise in residential areas** (Airbnb, quiet hotels) | Keep it quiet after about 21:00, and **sort your rubbish** as instructed (burnable / plastic / cans / bottles). |
| **No towel after washing your hands**: many public toilets have no paper towels or dryers. | Carry a handkerchief. |
| **Tattoos at onsen, gyms and pools** | Check the policy before you go, or look for tattoo-friendly or private baths. |
| **Leaving your passport at the hotel**: visitors must carry it, and tax-free shopping needs it. | Keep it with you, in a secure pocket. |

## 6. Safety

Japan is very safe, but natural hazards are real:
- **Earthquakes**: install **Safety tips** (JNTO's official app) for alerts in English. In a quake, protect your head, stay away from windows and follow staff instructions.
- **Heatstroke** (*necchūshō*) in July–August: drink water, use shade and take breaks.
- **Lost property** is usually handed in: ask at the station office or the nearest **kōban** (交番, police box).
- Emergency numbers: **110** police · **119** ambulance & fire · **050-3816-2787** Japan Visitor Hotline.
