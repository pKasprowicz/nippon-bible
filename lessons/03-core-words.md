# Lesson 3: Core Words

About 100 words that cover most of a tourist's day.

## 1. Numbers

| | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 ichi | 2 ni | 3 san | 4 **yon** | 5 go |
| 6 roku | 7 **nana** | 8 hachi | 9 **kyū** | 10 jū |

Alternative forms exist (4 *shi*, 7 *shichi*, 9 *ku*). *Shi* also means
"death", so people often avoid it. Use the bold forms.

**Building bigger numbers** works like a calculator:
- 11 = jū-ichi (10+1) · 20 = ni-jū (2×10) · 35 = san-jū-go
- 100 **hyaku** · 1,000 **sen** · **10,000 man** ← the key unit

| Price | Say | Note |
|-------|-----|------|
| ¥300 | san-**byaku** en | sound change |
| ¥600 | **roppyaku** en | sound change |
| ¥800 | **happyaku** en | sound change |
| ¥3,000 | san-**zen** en | sound change |
| ¥8,000 | **hassen** en | sound change |
| ¥10,000 | **ichi-man** en | not "jū-sen"! |
| ¥15,000 | ichi-man go-sen en | 1 man + 5 sen |
| ¥100,000 | **jū-man** en | 10 × man |

In practice, the price appears on the till screen, so focus on **understanding**
rather than saying numbers. Japanese counts in units of 10,000 (*man*), so
¥120,000 is "12 man", not "120 thousand".

## 2. Counting things and people

Japanese uses counters. The **universal counter** works for almost any
object (dishes, tickets, items in a shop):

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hitotsu | futatsu | mittsu | yottsu | itsutsu |

*Kore o **futatsu** kudasai.* · Two of these, please.

**People** (for restaurant tables):

| 1 person | 2 people | 3 people | 4 people | 5 people |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **hitori** | **futari** | san-nin | **yo-nin** | go-nin |

Holding up fingers works too.

## 3. Pointing words: this / that / where

| | near me | near you | over there | which? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thing | **kore** this | **sore** that | **are** that over there | **dore** which one |
| place | **koko** here | **soko** there | **asoko** over there | **doko** where |
| + noun | **kono** ~ this ~ | **sono** ~ | **ano** ~ | **dono** ~ |

*Kore* + pointing gets you a long way: **Kore o kudasai.** (This one, please.)

## 6. The polite *o-* / *go-*

You'll hear **o-** before many words (*o-cha, o-kane, o-kaikei, o-tera*). It
makes the word polite or softer. It doesn't change the meaning, so if you
hear *o-mizu*, that's still just *mizu*.
