# Lesson 6: Subways in Tokyo & Osaka

Both cities have huge networks, but they're logical once you know how the
signs work. Plan routes with Google Maps or a transit app, and use this lesson
to understand what you see in the station.

## 1. How every Japanese subway works

### Lines have a colour and a letter; stations have a number
Each line has a **colour** and a **letter**. Each station on it has a
**number**, so **G09** is stop 9 on the **G**inza line. Station codes are
printed on every sign, map and platform. If you can't read a name, match the code.

At a transfer station, each line gives it its own code. Tokyo's Ōtemachi, for
example, is M18, T09, C11, Z08 and I09.

### Your route in five steps
1. **Find the line colour/letter** and follow the signs for it (乗り場 *noriba* = boarding area).
2. **Tap your IC card** at the gate (改札 *kaisatsu*).
3. **Pick the right platform direction**: signs list the next stops and the
   end of the line (〜方面 *-hōmen* = towards ~).
4. **Check the train's destination** on the front of the train or the platform
   display (〜行 *-iki* / *-yuki* = bound for ~). Also check the type: 各停 local,
   急行 express. Express trains skip stations.
5. **Choose your exit before you leave the platform.** Yellow signs show exit
   numbers (A1, B3…) and the landmarks next to each exit.

### Words on the platform display
| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|----------|---------|---------|
| 〜行 | -iki / -yuki | bound for ~ |
| 先発 / 次発 | senpatsu / jihatsu | first train / next train |
| 当駅始発 | tōeki shihatsu | starts at this station (seats available!) |
| 各停 · 普通 | kakutei · futsū | local, all stops |
| 乗換 | norikae | transfer |
| 出口 | deguchi | exit |
| 女性専用車 | josei sen'yōsha | women-only car (rush hours, marked in pink) |
| 優先席 | yūsenseki | priority seats (elderly, pregnant, disabled) |
| 終電 | shūden | last train |

### Paying
- **IC card** (Suica, PASMO, ICOCA, any of them) works on **every** subway,
  JR and private line in both cities, and the right fare is deducted
  automatically. iPhone users can add Suica, PASMO or ICOCA to Apple Wallet.
- **Paper ticket**: if you can't work out the fare, buy the **cheapest** ticket and
  pay the difference at the **精算機** (*seisanki*, fare adjustment machine)
  before the exit gate. This is normal and expected.
- **Day passes** pay off if you take about 4 or more rides a day (see below).

### Timing
- **Rush hours**: about 7:30–9:30 and 17:30–19:30 on weekdays. Avoid travelling with big suitcases then.
- **Last trains** leave between about 23:30 and 0:30. Check the time of your *last connection*, not just the last train on the first line.
- First trains: around 5:00.

## 2. Tokyo

### Who runs what
Tokyo has **two subway companies** plus **JR** and many private railways:

| Operator | Lines |
|----------|-------|
| **Tokyo Metro** (9 lines) | Ginza **G** (orange) · Marunouchi **M** (red) · Hibiya **H** (silver) · Tōzai **T** (sky blue) · Chiyoda **C** (green) · Yūrakuchō **Y** (gold) · Hanzōmon **Z** (purple) · Namboku **N** (emerald) · Fukutoshin **F** (brown) |
| **Toei Subway** (4 lines) | Asakusa **A** (rose) · Mita **I** (blue) · Shinjuku **S** (leaf green) · Ōedo **E** (magenta) |
| **JR East** | **Yamanote line** (green loop, *JY*), linking Tokyo, Shinagawa, Shibuya, Shinjuku, Ikebukuro, Ueno, Akihabara · Chūō line (orange, *JC*) cutting across the loop |
| **Private railways** | Odakyū, Keiō, Tōkyū, Seibu, Tōbu, Keisei… run from the big hubs out to the suburbs |

### Tokyo tips
- **Metro ↔ Toei transfers** sometimes go through an extra gate marked
  **乗換改札** or with an orange "transfer" sign. Tap your card there. Don't walk out
  through a normal exit gate, or you'll start a new journey and pay twice.
- **Through-running**: many subway trains continue onto private railways
  under a different name. That's why the displayed destination can be a town you've never
  heard of. Check that the train **stops at your station**, especially if it's an express.
- **Same name, different stations**: "Shinjuku" means several separate stations
  (JR, Metro, Toei, Odakyū, Keiō, Seibu-Shinjuku) linked by long corridors. Allow
  10–15 minutes to transfer at Shinjuku, Shibuya, Tokyo and Ikebukuro.
- **Tokyo Station** has only one subway line, the Marunouchi. For other lines,
  walk underground to **Ōtemachi** (5 lines) or **Nihombashi**.
- **Pass:** the **Tokyo Subway Ticket** (24, 48 or 72 hours) covers all Tokyo
  Metro *and* Toei lines but **not JR**. It's sold to foreign visitors at
  airports, some stations and hotels. Bring your passport.
- **Airports**: from Narita, take the Keisei Skyliner (to Ueno/Nippori) or JR N'EX (to Tokyo/Shinjuku). From Haneda, take the Keikyū line (connects to Toei Asakusa) or the Tokyo Monorail (to JR Hamamatsuchō).

## 3. Osaka

### Lines
**Osaka Metro** has 8 lines plus the New Tram. The one you'll use most is the
**Midōsuji line (M, red)**, the city's north–south spine:

```
Shin-Ōsaka ── Umeda ── Shinsaibashi ── Namba ── Tennōji
(Shinkansen)  (JR Ōsaka) (shopping)    (Dōtonbori) (Abeno Harukas)
```

| Line | Use it for |
|------|-----------|
| **Midōsuji M** (red) | Shin-Osaka Shinkansen station, Umeda, Shinsaibashi, Namba, Tennōji |
| **Chūō C** (green) | Osaka Castle area (Tanimachi 4-chōme / Morinomiya), Osaka Bay & Aquarium (Ōsakakō) |
| **Sakaisuji K** (brown) | Nippombashi (Den Den Town), Ebisuchō (Tsūtenkaku, Shinsekai) |
| **Tanimachi T** (purple) · **Yotsubashi Y** (blue) · **Sennichimae S** (pink) · **Nagahori Tsurumi-ryokuchi N** (yellow-green) · **Imazatosuji I** (orange) | Other districts |
| **JR Osaka Loop line** (orange, JR) | Circles the centre: Osaka, Tennōji, Osakajōkōen (castle). Universal Studios via the JR Yumesaki line |

### Osaka tips
- **One area, many names**: **Umeda** (Metro) = **Ōsaka** (JR) = **Ōsaka-Umeda**
  (Hankyū, Hanshin). They're all the same district, linked by a vast underground maze. Higashi-Umeda (T) and Nishi-Umeda (Y) are separate stations nearby. Allow extra time and follow the line colours.
- **Namba is several stations too**: Osaka Metro Namba, Nankai Namba (trains to Kansai Airport), Kintetsu/Hanshin Ōsaka-Namba (trains to Nara) and JR Namba.
- **Escalators**: stand on the **right** in Osaka (left in Tokyo).
- **Contactless bank cards**: Osaka Metro gates also take Visa/Mastercard
  contactless cards, which is handy if you don't have an IC card.
- **Passes:** the **Enjoy Eco Card** (1-day Osaka Metro pass, cheaper at weekends) and
  the **Osaka Amazing Pass** (transport plus free entry to many sights).
- **Kansai Airport (KIX)**: Nankai line to Namba (the "rapi:t" is the limited express), or JR *Haruka* to Tennōji, Ōsaka, Shin-Osaka and Kyoto.
- Your **Suica/PASMO works in Osaka**, and ICOCA works in Tokyo.

## 4. Useful phrases in the station

| Japanese | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **[X]-sen wa doko desu ka?** | Where is the X line? |
| **[X] ni ikitai desu.** | I want to go to X. |
| **Kono densha wa [X] ni tomarimasu ka?** | Does this train stop at X? |
| **Nan-ban deguchi desu ka?** | Which exit number? |
| **Norikae wa doko desu ka?** | Where do I transfer? |
| **Kippu o nakushimashita.** | I lost my ticket. |
| **Kādo no zandaka ga tarimasen.** | My card balance is too low. (staff point you to チャージ) |

If something goes wrong at the gate (it beeps and closes), step back and go
to the **staffed window** (有人改札 *yūjin kaisatsu*) next to the gates.
