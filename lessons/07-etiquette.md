# Lesson 7: Customs, Etiquette, Do's & Don'ts

Japanese etiquette rests on three ideas: **don't inconvenience others**
(*meiwaku o kakenai*), **keep inside and outside separate** (shoes, dirt,
noise) and **show respect through form**. Visitors aren't expected to be
perfect, but making an effort is noticed and appreciated.

## 1. Meeting people

- **Bowing**: as a visitor, a slight bow or nod with a smile is right. Return
  a bow with a bow. A handshake is fine if the other person offers one.
- **Names**: add **-san** to *other people's* names (Tanaka-san), never to your own.
  Family name usually comes first.
- **Business cards** (*meishi*): give and take them with **both hands** and read the card
  for a moment. Don't write on it or put it in your back pocket.
- **Physical contact**: hugs, cheek kisses and back slaps aren't usual. Keep a little more distance than in southern Europe.
- **Saying no**: people rarely say a direct "no". *Chotto…* ("it's a bit…"),
  *kentō shimasu* ("I'll consider it") or a sharp intake of breath usually **mean no**.
- **Pointing**: point with an open hand rather than one finger. To call someone
  over, wave with the palm **down**.
- **Voice**: speak at a lower volume than at home, especially indoors and on transport.

## 2. Visiting a home

- Take your **shoes off** in the entrance area (*genkan*), pointing them toward the door.
  Step up onto the raised floor in socks, or in the slippers you're offered.
- **Slippers off before tatami.** Never wear the **toilet slippers** outside the toilet. It's a classic tourist mistake.
- Bring a small **gift** (*omiyage*), ideally something from your home country or region, and give it with both hands. Avoid sets of **4** (*shi* sounds like "death") and **9** (*ku* sounds like "suffering").
- Say **o-jama shimasu** ("sorry to intrude") as you enter and **o-jama shimashita** as you leave.

## 3. At the table

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Say *itadakimasu* before and *gochisōsama deshita* after | Start eating or drinking before everyone has been served or the *kanpai* |
| Lift the rice or soup bowl close to your mouth | Lean down to the table to eat |
| **Slurp** noodles; it's a compliment | Blow your nose at the table |
| Pour drinks for others and let them fill your glass | Fill your own glass when you're in a group |
| Rest chopsticks on the holder or across your bowl's edge | **Stick chopsticks upright in rice** or **pass food chopstick to chopstick** (both are funeral rites) |
| Eat nigiri sushi with your fingers if you like, dipping the **fish side** in soy sauce | Point with chopsticks, stab food, or wave them over dishes deciding what to take |
| Use the reverse end of your chopsticks to take from shared plates (if no serving chopsticks) | Pour soy sauce over plain rice or soak sushi in it |
| Finish your plate; it shows appreciation | Ask for changes to a set course or chef's choice (*omakase*) |

- **Paying:** at the till by the door, and no tipping. Groups often split the bill evenly (*warikan*).
- **Ekiben** (station lunch boxes) are for long-distance trains, not commuter trains.

## 4. In public places

### On the street
- **No eating while walking.** Eat by the stall, or stand next to the vending machine you bought from.
- **No smoking** on most streets. Use designated smoking areas (喫煙所). Some Tokyo wards fine people for smoking on the street.
- **Take your rubbish with you.** Public bins are rare, but konbini and stations have some.
- **Queue** in orderly lines: for trains, lifts, popular restaurants and taxis.
- Wait for the green light even on empty streets, and keep to the side of the pavement the locals use.

### On trains & buses
- Phone on **silent** (*manā mōdo*, "manner mode") and **no phone calls**.
- Wear your backpack on your **front** or put it on the rack in crowded trains.
- Leave **priority seats** (優先席) free for those who need them.
- Let people off before you board, and line up at the marked spots.

### Temples & shrines
- These are places of worship, not theme parks. Speak quietly and dress modestly.
- At shrines, walk to the **side** of the central path under the torii. The centre is said to be for the gods.
- **Shrine prayer:** rinse your hands and mouth at the *temizuya* (don't drink from the ladle), drop a coin, then **bow twice, clap twice, bow once**.
- **Temple prayer:** bow with your palms together, **no clapping**.
- Look for signs before taking photos (撮影禁止 = no photos). Never photograph people praying.
- *Goshuin* (temple stamps) go in a proper stamp book, never on loose paper or in your passport.

### Onsen & sentō (public baths)
1. Undress fully in the changing room. Swimwear isn't allowed.
2. **Wash thoroughly** at the shower stools *before* getting in.
3. Keep the small towel **out of the water** (fold it on your head) and tie up long hair.
4. Don't swim, splash, or dive in. Talk quietly.
5. Dry off before returning to the changing room.
6. **Tattoos** are often refused. Look for "tattoo-friendly" baths, cover small tattoos with stickers if allowed, or book a private bath (*kashikiri-buro*).

### Toilets
- Japanese "washlet" toilets have a control panel. Read the buttons: **止** stop · **おしり** rear wash · **ビデ** bidet · **流す** / **大** / **小** flush (big / small).
- Many public toilets have no paper towels: carry a small towel or handkerchief, as locals do.
- Use the separate toilet slippers if they're provided, and take them off when you leave.

### Shops & service
- Staff greet you (*irasshaimase*), but you don't have to reply.
- Put money and cards on the **tray**, not in the cashier's hand.
- **Don't haggle**, except perhaps at flea markets.
- Handle goods carefully, and ask before trying on clothes (*shichaku shite mo ii desu ka?*).

## 5. Do's & Don'ts at a glance

### ✅ Do
- Learn and use **sumimasen**, **arigatō gozaimasu** and **onegaishimasu**.
- Wear **easy slip-on shoes** and socks without holes; you'll take your shoes off often.
- **Carry cash**, a handkerchief and a small bag for your rubbish.
- **Queue**, keep to the side, and let people off the train first.
- Give and receive things (money, cards, gifts) with **both hands**.
- **Follow local signs and staff instructions**, even when they seem strict.
- **Respect closing times** and *last order* (L.O.) times in restaurants.
- Carry your **passport**: foreign visitors must have it on them at all times.

### ❌ Don't
- **Tip.** It isn't done.
- Talk loudly or make **phone calls on public transport**.
- **Eat while walking** or smoke outside designated areas.
- Stick **chopsticks upright** in rice or pass food chopstick to chopstick.
- Wear **shoes** on tatami or indoors where others take them off.
- Get into an **onsen** without washing first, or put your towel in the water.
- **Photograph** people (especially geiko and maiko in Kyoto) without permission, or go into private alleys.
- **Blow your nose** at the table or loudly in public. Step aside, or into a toilet.
- **Point** with your chopsticks or your index finger at people.
- **Litter**, or leave rubbish at sightseeing spots.
- Bring **medicines** into Japan without checking the rules. Some common European cold and ADHD medicines are banned (see Lesson 8).
