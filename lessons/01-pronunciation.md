# Lesson 1: Pronunciation

Japanese pronunciation is **easier for Europeans than English**. Spelling is
regular, there are only five vowels, and if you speak Italian, Spanish or
Polish, most sounds already exist in your language.

## 1. Five pure vowels

| Romaji | Sounds like | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **a** | Italian *casa* | **a**me 雨 (rain) |
| **i** | Italian *vino* | **i**nu 犬 (dog) |
| **u** | German *u* but with lips **relaxed, not rounded** | **u**mi 海 (sea) |
| **e** | Spanish *mesa* (short, never "ee") | **e**ki 駅 (station) |
| **o** | Italian *otto* (short) | **o**cha お茶 (green tea) |

Rules:
- Vowels stay pure. No English-style glides, so **e** never becomes "ay" and **o** never becomes "ow".
- **Every vowel is pronounced**, including a final *e*: *sake* = **sa-ke**,
  *karaoke* = **ka-ra-o-ke**, *Kobe* = **Ko-be**.

### The silent-ish *u* and *i*
Between voiceless consonants (k, s, t, h, p), and at the end of *desu / -masu*,
**u** and **i** are often whispered or dropped:

- desu です → "**dess**" · arimasu あります → "a-ri-**mass**"
- sukiyaki すきやき → "**ski**-ya-ki" · hitotsu ひとつ → "h'to-tsu"

## 2. Long vowels change meaning

A long vowel lasts **twice as long**. Treat it as a real difference:

| Short | Long |
|-------|------|
| obasan おばさん · aunt | ob**ā**san おばあさん · grandmother |
| ojisan おじさん · uncle / middle-aged man | oj**ī**san おじいさん · grandfather |
| biru ビル · building | b**ī**ru ビール · beer |
| shujin しゅじん · husband | sh**ū**jin しゅうじん · prisoner |

Spelling tips: **ou** and **oo** read as a long *ō* (*Toukyou* = Tōkyō).
**ei** usually reads as a long *ē* (*sensei* ≈ "sen-sē").

## 3. Consonants to watch

| Sound | How | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| **r** | One quick tap of the tongue, between l, r and d (Spanish *pero*). Never rolled, never English "r". | **r**āmen ラーメン, a**r**igatō ありがとう |
| **f** | Soft breath between nearly closed lips, no teeth | **f**uji ふじ, tō**f**u とうふ |
| **ts** | As in German *Zeit* or Italian *pizza* | **ts**unami つなみ, **ts**ukue つくえ |
| **ch / j / sh** | As in English *church / jam / ship* | **ch**a ちゃ, **j**inja じんじゃ, **sh**ashin しゃしん |
| **g** | Always hard, as in *go* | **g**inkō ぎんこう (bank) |
| **y** | Like German *j* | **y**ama やま (mountain); *yen* is actually said "en" えん |
| **w** | Light, almost no lip rounding | **w**asabi わさび |

### Double consonants (pause!)
A doubled consonant is a **short silent beat**, like the gap in Italian *fatto*:

- ki**t**e きて (come) vs ki**tt**e きって (postage stamp)
- o**t**o おと (sound) vs o**tt**o おっと (husband)
- i**pp**on いっぽん (one bottle) · ki**pp**u きっぷ (ticket) · za**ss**hi ざっし (magazine)

### The syllable *n*
*n* at the end of a syllable counts as **one beat** by itself:
*ko-n-ni-chi-wa* こんにちは (5 beats), *Ni-ho-n* にほん (3 beats), *ra-a-me-n* ラーメン (4 beats).
Before *p, b, m* it sounds like *m*: *shi**n**bun* しんぶん → "shimbun" (newspaper).

## 4. Rhythm & melody

- **Every beat (mora) has the same length**, a bit like a machine gun:
  *To-o-kyo-o* (4 beats), *O-o-sa-ka* (4 beats), *Kyo-o-to* (3 beats).
- **No strong stress.** Don't punch a syllable like *ToKYO*. Keep it flat and even.
- Japanese has a pitch accent (high/low tones), but at this level flat and
  even speech is understood fine.

### Common traveller mistakes

| Don't say | Say |
|-----------|-----|
| "Kee-YO-toe" | **Kyō-to** 京都: *kyo* is one beat, then *o* again (long) |
| "Ka-ree-OH-kee" | **ka-ra-o-ke** カラオケ |
| "Sah-KEE" | **sa-ke** 酒 |
| "Hi-ROSH-ima" (heavy stress) | **Hi-ro-shi-ma** 広島, all even |
| "Ah-ree-GAH-toe" | **a-ri-ga-tō** ありがとう, tapped *r*, long *ō* |

## 5. Quick drill

Read aloud, clapping once per beat. On the website, tap the Japanese to hear it:

1. ありがとうございます *arigatō gozaimasu* (a-ri-ga-to-o go-za-i-ma-s) – 10 beats
2. すみません *sumimasen* (su-mi-ma-se-n) – 5 beats
3. きっぷ *kippu* (ki-p-pu) – 3 beats
4. しんかんせん *Shinkansen* (shi-n-ka-n-se-n) – 6 beats
5. おねがいします *onegaishimasu* (o-ne-ga-i-shi-ma-s) – 7 beats
