# Hiragana 1: The Vowels あいうえお

Every Japanese syllable ends in one of five vowels, and these five characters are
those vowels on their own. Learn them well: every later unit builds on them.

**Your mission:** by the end of this unit you can read **いいえ** (no), **いい** (good)
and **うえ** (up), the kind of words you'll see on lift buttons and hear every day.

## 1. Guess first

You haven't been taught these yet. Guess anyway: a wrong guess now makes the right
answer easier to remember in a minute.

```quiz
title: Guess first
? Which one do you think is "i"? Hint: it looks like two letters i without dots.
- あ
- い *
- お
> い is two short strokes standing side by side, like "ii".

? あ and お look alike. One of them has an extra little stroke. Which is "o"?
- あ
- お *
> お has the extra dot flying off on the right: "Oh! I dropped something."
```

## 2. Meet the characters

Tap each card to hear it. For each one: **look**, **listen**, say it **out loud**, then
picture the memory aid for two seconds. Trace the shape in the air with your finger if
it helps; movement adds another route into memory.

```cards
kana: あいうえお
```

The vowels sound like **Italian or Spanish vowels**: short and pure (see
[Lesson 1: Pronunciation](../lessons/01-pronunciation.md)).

## 3. Practice

Recognition first, then recall. Mistakes come back a few questions later.

```practice
kana: あいうえお
modes: see,pick,listen,type
count: 15
```

## 4. Tell them apart

```contrast
あ お | お has a small extra stroke at the top right; あ doesn't.
い り | Both are two strokes. In り the right stroke is much longer and the strokes join.
```

The pair い/り uses り (ri) from Unit 5. You don't need to read it yet; just learn that
the **short, separate** strokes are い.

## 5. Read real words

These words use only the five vowels. Read each one **aloud** first, then type it.

```words
いいえ | iie | no
いい | ii | good
うえ | ue | up, above (on lift buttons as 上)
あお | ao | blue
いえ | ie | house
あい | ai | love
え | e | picture
あおい | aoi | blue (as an adjective)
```

Notice **いい** vs **いいえ**: doubling a vowel makes it long, and length changes the word.
