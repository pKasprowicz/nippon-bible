# Nippon Bible

Japanese for European travellers, in eight parts:

| Part | What it is | Start |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **Japan guide** | 10 lessons: pronunciation, words, phrases, situations, subways, etiquette, pitfalls, food, and a cheat sheet with a self-test | [Overview](lessons/00-guide.md) |
| **Hiragana course** | Learn to read hiragana (えき, すし, ありがとう) in 8 interactive units, with spaced daily review and an endless adaptive game | [Start here](hiragana/00-start.md) |
| **Katakana course** | Learn to read katakana, the script of foreign words (ホテル, コーヒー, タクシー), in 8 interactive units, with the same exercises, review and game | [Start here](katakana/00-start.md) |
| **Kana test** | Tests only, mixing hiragana and katakana: reading (choose or type), listening, twin pairs (あ ↔ ア), look-alikes (シ/ツ, ぬ/め, り/リ) and real words, in four levels (basic, voiced, combinations with small っ and ー, words) and a 40-question final exam. A report after each test colours every character by result and links the weakest to the unit that teaches them | [Start here](kanatest/00-start.md) |
| **Kanji course** | The 95 kanji on signs in stations, restaurants, shops, toilets, onsen and public places (出口, 駅, 禁煙), with their meanings and readings, in 9 interactive units organised by place | [Start here](kanji/00-start.md) |
| **Japanese cuisine handbook** | 14 long-form chapters on how Japanese food is made: umami and dashi, kōji, soy sauce, miso, mirin and vinegar, raw fish, frying, kōji marinades, rice, noodles, tofu, bean paste and agar, mochi and starches, and texture vocabulary. Every fact is cited from research papers or official bodies; freely licensed photos, molecule diagrams and Wikipedia links for further reading; a quiz per chapter and a glossary | [Start here](cuisine/00-start.md) |
| **Folklore handbook: kami, ghosts and yōkai** | 13 long-form chapters on Japanese belief: kami and the word "Shinto", the myths and the Land of Yomi, pollution and purification, a thousand years of kami and buddhas together and their separation in 1868, ancestors and Bon, vengeful spirits, how the white-robed yūrei took shape, a field guide to the kinds of ghost, and a separate part on yōkai: definitions, night parades and tool spirits, a bestiary, and yōkai in modern Japan. Cited from peer-reviewed scholarship, public-domain primary texts and official statistics; woodblock prints, scrolls, photographs and explanatory diagrams; a quiz per chapter and a glossary | [Start here](folklore/00-start.md) |
| **Pop culture handbook: manga, anime and beyond** | 14 long-form chapters: picture scrolls, Edo picture books and how *manga* got its name; manga and the weekly magazine system; anime and the production committee; manga symbols and role language; cinema from the golden age to Kore-eda, Hamaguchi and *Kokuhō*; Godzilla and special effects; video games; enka, idols, city pop and Hatsune Miku; kawaii and local mascots; Comiket, Akihabara and cosplay; anime pilgrimage and folklore in pop culture; Cool Japan; a traveller's guide with dated official rules; and the Japanese of fiction for learners. Cited from scholarship and official data; public-domain prints, photographs and diagrams, but no copyrighted characters; a quiz per chapter and a glossary | [Start here](popculture/00-start.md) |
| **Bathing handbook: onsen, sentō and ryokan** | 12 long-form chapters: what the Hot Springs Act counts as an onsen, where the heat comes from and the onsen map in numbers, how to read the spring label and the notices about added water and recirculation; temple charity baths and Zen bath halls, the bathhouses of Edo and the bans on mixed bathing, the modern sentō and its decline; Kusatsu, Beppu and Dōgo and the curative stay (*tōji*), staying at a ryokan, bath etiquette, tattoos, the Ministry of the Environment's health precautions, and the Japanese at the door and the front desk. Cited from laws, official data and scholarship; freely licensed photographs (no bathers) and diagrams; a quiz per chapter and a glossary | [Start here](bathing/00-start.md) |
| **Railways handbook: from steam to Shinkansen** | 13 long-form chapters: the first line of 1872 and the private railway boom; nationalisation and the founding of JNR; the Shinkansen from the 1939 "bullet train" plan to today; the break-up of JNR in 1987; Hankyu and the railways that built suburbs; the subways of Tokyo and Osaka; safety and punctuality; the Shinkansen network and the maglev; fares, IC cards and the Japan Rail Pass with dated prices and a disclaimer; local lines, freight and the Sunrise Express; ekiben; and railway Japanese. Cited from MLIT, the railway companies, the National Diet Library and scholarship; public-domain prints and freely licensed photographs; diagrams, a quiz per chapter and a glossary | [Start here](railways/00-start.md) |
| **Festivals and seasons handbook: the Japanese year** | 12 long-form chapters: the old lunisolar calendar, the switch of 1873 and era names; the 24 solar terms, *rokuyō* lucky days and old month names; the 1948 holiday law, the 16 national holidays, Golden Week and 2026–2027 dates; the five *sekku*; New Year and the first shrine visit; *setsubun* and how the Japan Meteorological Agency measures the cherry blossom; Bon, the *furyū-odori* dances and fireworks; the harvest moon, the red-leaf front and Shichi-Go-San; what a *matsuri* is and the 37 float festivals on the UNESCO list; Gion, Tenjin, Kanda, Nebuta, Awa Odori and the Sapporo Snow Festival, with dates and a disclaimer; how to join in and stay safe in crowds; and festival Japanese. Cited from the Cabinet Office, the National Diet Library, the National Astronomical Observatory, the JMA, the Agency for Cultural Affairs, UNESCO, cities and organisers, and scholarship; public-domain prints and freely licensed photographs; diagrams, a quiz per chapter and a glossary | [Start here](festivals/00-start.md) |
| **Picture dictionary: words you can see** | 19 drawn scenes with about 220 everyday words and 70 phrases: positions and directions; a set meal, ramen, sushi and tastes; a convenience store and a restaurant; a station and a hotel; the body and a pharmacy; money and counters; clocks and the week; a town street; a Japanese-style room and the bath. Tap anything to hear it and see it in kanji and kana (romaji on request); quiz yourself with *Find it* and *Name it*; missed words join the daily review of the kana and kanji courses | [Start here](pictures/00-start.md) |
| **Signs in the wild: photo gallery** | Photographs of Japanese text in public space (station boards, warnings and house rules, notices, street signs, shop signs and product labels) with the main text, a translation and a credit for each. Every photo is public domain or CC0 | [Gallery](signs/00-gallery.md) |

**Read it online:** <https://pkasprowicz.github.io/nippon-bible/>. The website adds
tap-to-listen audio for all Japanese text (recorded, so it sounds the same on every device and
works offline), the interactive exercises, progress tracking and a flashcard drill. On GitHub, the kana courses' exercises show as plain text.

**Use it offline, as an app:** the website saves itself on your device the first time you open it,
then works with no connection. On Android, choose *Install app* in Chrome's ⋮ menu (or the
*Install the app* link on the home page) to get a launcher icon; on iPhone, use Safari's
*Share › Add to Home Screen*. See *Install & offline* in the site's menu.

**Search everything:** the magnifier in the site's top bar (or the `/` key) searches every lesson,
course and handbook at once, in English, romaji, kana or kanji, and opens the matching section.
It works offline too.

**Tap to look up:** tap any Japanese word or phrase to hear it and see its reading and meaning;
tap a romaji term, a chemical substance (English and Polish name, formula and a PubChem abstract),
a linked name, a citation such as *(Kurihara 2015)* or a chapter link for a short pop-up.

No previous knowledge needed. Japanese is given as **script · romaji · meaning**, e.g.
`駅 · eki · station`, with Hepburn romaji (a macron marks a long vowel: **ō** = "oo").
