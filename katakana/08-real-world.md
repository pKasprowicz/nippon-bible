You know every katakana. This unit puts you in front of what you'll actually see:
café menus, shop names, hotel and station signs, and the names of European countries
and cities.

### European words hiding in Japanese

Not every katakana word is English. Some arrived centuries ago from Portuguese, Dutch,
German or French:

| Katakana | From | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| パン · *pan* | Portuguese *pão* | bread |
| タバコ · *tabako* | Portuguese *tabaco* | cigarettes |
| アルバイト · *arubaito* | German *Arbeit* | part-time job |
| カルテ · *karute* | German *Karte* | medical record |
| アンケート · *ankēto* | French *enquête* | questionnaire |
| ビール · *bīru* | Dutch *bier* | beer |

## 6. Your name in katakana

Foreign names are written in katakana by sound. Can you recognise these common European names?

```quiz
title: Whose name is it?
? マリア
- Maria *
- Marie
- Mario
> ma-ri-a. Marie would be マリー (ma-rī), with a long ī.

? トーマス
- Thomas *
- Tomasz
- Thomson
> tō-ma-su. English th becomes t or s, and a final s gets a u.

? ソフィー
- Sophie *
- Sofia
- Soph
> so-fī: フィ is the extra sound "fi". Sofia would be ソフィア.

? アンナ
- Anna *
- Anne
- Ana
> a-n-na: a double n is written ンナ.
```

**Try it yourself:** split your name into syllables, match each to katakana, add ー for long
vowels and ッ before doubled consonants. Japanese staff will be delighted if you can read it on a booking.
