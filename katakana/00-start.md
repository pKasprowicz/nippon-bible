# Katakana Course: Start Here

Katakana is Japan's second "alphabet". It has the same 46 sounds as hiragana, drawn with
sharper, straighter strokes, and it's used mainly for **words borrowed from other
languages**. That's great news for a European: once you can sound out
**コーヒー** (kōhī), you already know it means *coffee*.

Katakana is everywhere a traveller looks: menus (**ラーメン**, **ビール**), shops
(**コンビニ**, **ドラッグストア**), hotels (**フロント**, **チェックイン**), stations
(**エレベーター**, **コインロッカー**), and brands (**ユニクロ**, **セブンイレブン**).

This course gets you **reading** katakana at a traveller's level in about **8 sessions of
20–30 minutes**, plus **5 minutes of review a day**. It works on its own, but it's easier
if you've done the [Hiragana course](../hiragana/00-start.md) first: many katakana look
like their hiragana twins (か/カ, へ/ヘ, り/リ).

## 1. What you'll be able to do

- Recognise all 46 basic katakana, the **゛/゜** variants, combinations like **シャ**, and the extra sounds for foreign words (**ティ**, **ファ**, **チェ**)
- Read the long-vowel bar **ー** and the small **ッ** that change a word's sound
- **Decode loanwords**: turn **レストラン** into *restaurant* and **タクシー** into *taxi*
- Read menus, shop names, hotel and station signs, and the katakana name of your own country

## 2. How this course works

It uses the same research-based techniques as the Hiragana course: **self-testing**,
**spaced review**, **memory aids with sound**, **guessing first**, **mixing old and new**,
**look-alike contrast**, **practice that gets harder** (read → find → listen → type),
**speed rounds**, and an **endless adaptive game**. The
[Hiragana start page](../hiragana/00-start.md) explains each one, with references.

Three things are new for katakana:

| Technique | Why | How |
|-----------|-----|-----|
| **Transfer from what you know** | New knowledge sticks better when it's linked to old knowledge. | Memory aids link each katakana to its hiragana twin, or to the kanji it came from (二, 三, 口). |
| **Meaning first** | Words you can understand are far easier to remember than random sounds. | "Guess the word" rounds: sound out a loanword, then pick its meaning. |
| **Decoding rules** | A few patterns explain how English, French or German words turn into katakana. | Unit 2 teaches the rules, and every later unit uses them. |

> **The trickiest part of katakana** is a handful of look-alike pairs: **シ/ツ**, **ソ/ン**,
> **ク/ケ/タ**, **コ/ユ**, **ル/レ**. Every unit has a "Tell them apart" drill for them.

## 4. How to use the exercises

- **Say every answer out loud.** For loanwords, say the katakana reading first, then the original word.
- **Tap any Japanese text** to hear it. If you hear nothing, your device may need a Japanese voice.
- **Keyboard:** <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd> to answer, <kbd>Enter</kbd> to continue, <kbd>R</kbd> to replay a sound.
- **Typing answers:** type the *Japanese* reading, not the English word: コーヒー is **kōhī** (or *koohii*, *kohi*), not "coffee".
- Your katakana progress is saved **in this browser only**, separately from hiragana.

## 5. Try it now: how much can you guess?

```quiz
title: Guess first
? Katakana is mostly used for…
- grammar endings
- words borrowed from other languages, and names *
- numbers
> Hiragana handles grammar, kanji carry meaning, and katakana spells foreign words and names: ホテル (hotel), ドイツ (Germany).

? Which of these is katakana?
- かさ
- カサ *
- 傘
> カサ is katakana (straight and angular). かさ is hiragana, and 傘 is kanji. All read "kasa", umbrella.

? タクシー is read "takushī". What does it mean?
- taxi *
- tax
- tuxedo
> Japanese adds vowels between consonants: ta-ku-shī = taxi. You'll learn this pattern in Unit 2.

? What do you think the bar ー in コーヒー does?
- it's a hyphen between two words
- it makes the vowel before it long *
- it's silent
> ー doubles the length of the vowel before it: kō-hī. It's the most useful katakana sign of all.
```
