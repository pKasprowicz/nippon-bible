# Kanji 1: Numbers and Prices

The easiest kanji, and some of the most useful: every price tag, menu and ticket machine
uses them. Most shops write prices in Arabic numerals, but traditional restaurants, market
stalls and handwritten menus often use kanji.

**Your mission:** read **千円**, **五百円** and **一万円**, and work out any price written in kanji.

## 1. Guess first

```quiz
title: Guess first
? 一, 二 and 三 are one, two and three strokes. What do you think 三 means?
- one
- three *
- ten
> 一 ichi, 二 ni, 三 san: count the strokes.

? 十 is a cross. Which number might it be?
- ten *
- plus
- four
> 十 (jū) = ten. Think of the Roman X turned upright.
```

## 6. Tell them apart

```contrast
二 三 | Count the strokes: 二 two, 三 three.
十 千 | 千 is 十 with a slash on top.
七 十 | 七's vertical stroke bends at the bottom; 十 is a straight cross.
八 六 | 六 is 八 with a lid on top.
```

## 7. How prices are written

Kanji numbers work like saying the number aloud: **digit, then its unit**.

| Kanji | How it's built | Reading | Value |
|-------|---------------|---------|-------|
| 三百円 | three × hundred, yen | *sanbyaku en* | ¥300 |
| 千二百円 | thousand + two × hundred | *sen nihyaku en* | ¥1,200 |
| 五千八百円 | five × thousand + eight × hundred | *gosen happyaku en* | ¥5,800 |
| 二万円 | two × ten-thousand | *niman en* | ¥20,000 |

Some sounds change: 三百 *sanbyaku*, 六百 *roppyaku*, 八百 *happyaku*, 三千 *sanzen*, 八千 *hassen*.
