# Kanji Course: Start Here

Kanji are the characters borrowed from Chinese. Japanese uses thousands of them, but the
signs a traveller needs use only a small, very regular set. This course teaches the **95 kanji
you'll meet most on signs** in stations, restaurants, shops, toilets, onsen and other public
places, and about **120 real sign words** built from them: **出口** (exit), **禁煙** (no
smoking), **税込** (tax included), **営業中** (open).

You'll learn each kanji's **meaning** *and* its **reading**: enough to understand a sign at a
glance and to say it when you ask for directions.

## 1. What you'll be able to do

- Find the right exit, platform and direction in a station: **北口**, **2番線**, **方面**, **改札**
- Read a menu's basics and use a meal-ticket machine: **定食**, **牛肉**, **生ビール**, **食券**
- Understand prices and shop signs: **千円**, **税込**, **割引**, **現金のみ**
- Check opening hours and closing days: **営業中**, **定休日**, **月曜日**
- Pick the right door: **男 / 女**, **男湯 / 女湯**, **使用中 / 空き**, **押 / 引**
- Spot warnings: **危険**, **注意**, **禁止**, **非常口**

## 2. How kanji differ from kana

**Meaning comes first.** A kanji is a symbol for an idea: 口 is a mouth, an opening. Put
入 (enter) in front and you get **入口**, "entering opening", the entrance. You can often
understand a sign without knowing how to say it.

**Readings change with the word.** Most kanji have two kinds of reading: a native Japanese
one (口 = *kuchi*) and one borrowed from Chinese (口 = *kō*). In compound words, sounds can
also change: 入 + 口 is *iri**g**uchi*, not *iri-kuchi*. That's why this course teaches readings
**through real sign words**, and each kanji card shows its main reading plus the others.
When you type a reading, any correct reading is accepted.

**Kanji are built from parts.** About 200 small building blocks recur everywhere: 氵 (water)
in 湯 *hot water*, 洗 *wash* and 温 *warm*; 木 (tree) in 休 *rest* and 本 *origin*. The memory aids
use these parts, so each new kanji gets easier.

## 3. How this course works

It uses the same research-based techniques as the kana courses (see the
[Hiragana start page](../hiragana/00-start.md) for the background and references):
guessing first, memory aids with sound, self-testing, practice that gets harder, mixing old and
new, look-alike drills, real-world reading, checkpoints, an endless adaptive game, and spaced
daily review.

What's specific to kanji:

| Technique | How it works here |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Meaning and reading** | Every kanji is practised both ways: what it means (pick the meaning, find the kanji for a meaning) and how it's read (pick or type the reading, recognise it by ear). Checkpoints ask both. |
| **Parts-based memory aids** | Each kanji's memory aid is built from its parts (亻 person + 木 tree = 休 rest), which also helps with kanji you haven't studied. |
| **Learning by place** | Units follow the places you'll visit, so each set of kanji arrives with the signs it belongs on. Later units reuse earlier kanji. |
| **Words in context** | Sign words come with the place you'd see them, and hints show each kanji's meaning rather than its sound. |

## 5. How to use the exercises

- **Say the meaning and the reading out loud** before you answer.
- **Typing readings:** type the reading in Latin letters (*deguchi*). Any correct reading of a single kanji counts; for sign words, type how the whole word is read.
- **Hints** on sign words show what each kanji means: work the reading out from there.
- **Keyboard:** <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd> to answer, <kbd>Enter</kbd> to continue, <kbd>R</kbd> to replay a sound.
- Your kanji progress is saved **in this browser only**, separately from kana. To continue on another device, use [Sync progress](#/progress).

## 6. Try it now: guess first

```quiz
title: Guess first
? 口 is a picture of a mouth. On a sign, 入口 combines "enter" + "opening". What is it?
- an entrance *
- a restaurant
- a ticket office
> 入口 (iriguchi) is the entrance. Its partner is 出口 (deguchi), the exit.

? 男 is a rice field (田) above strength (力). Which door is it on?
- the men's toilet *
- the women's toilet
- the staff room
> 男 (otoko) = man. 女 (onna) = woman, a kneeling figure.

? 円 appears after every price. What does ¥1,000 look like in kanji?
- 千円 *
- 十円
- 百円
> 千 (sen) = thousand, 円 (en) = yen: 千円 is ¥1,000.
```
