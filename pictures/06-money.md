# 6. Money and Counting

Japanese coins and notes, and the "counters" that Japanese uses when it counts things: a different word for flat things, long things, people, animals and machines.

**How to use the pictures.** In *Study*, tap anything to hear its name and see it written. *Find it* says a word and you tap the object; *Name it* circles an object and you choose its name. Words you miss go into your daily review in the [kana](../hiragana/00-start.md) and [kanji](../kanji/00-start.md) courses. Romaji is hidden until you switch it on.

## 6.1 Money

Coins are 〜円玉 (*-en-dama*), notes are 〜円札 (*-en-satsu*). 円 (*en*) is the yen.

### The all-purpose counter: one to ten

When you do not know the right counter, the native Japanese numbers with 〜つ work for most things, up to ten:

| Japanese | Kana | Romaji | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 一つ | ひとつ | *hitotsu* | one |
| 二つ | ふたつ | *futatsu* | two |
| 三つ | みっつ | *mittsu* | three |
| 四つ | よっつ | *yottsu* | four |
| 五つ | いつつ | *itsutsu* | five |
| 六つ | むっつ | *muttsu* | six |
| 七つ | ななつ | *nanatsu* | seven |
| 八つ | やっつ | *yattsu* | eight |
| 九つ | ここのつ | *kokonotsu* | nine |
| とお | とお | *tō* | ten (十; with this counter, read tō) |
| これを二つください | これをふたつください | *kore o futatsu kudasai* | Two of these, please |

? Which counter is used for tickets and sheets of paper?

? How do you say "two people"?
- 二人 (futari) *
- 二つ (futatsu)
- 二匹 (nihiki)
> 人 counts people; one and two are irregular: hitori, futari.

? What is お釣り?
- the change you get back *
